    How calculated · limitations

    Each loaded row is an incident, not necessarily a unique soldier or a conviction. Duplicate rows/names are retained and noted here. All four filters apply to every analytical section. Search affects the soldier table only.

    DY means training year (October–September). Incident Date is expected and drives the timing charts. Date/OWI Date are accepted aliases. If DY itself holds a full date and Incident Date is blank, use it as a legacy incident-date fallback and derive TY. A numeric year (2027, 27, TY27) is never interpreted as an Excel date. An explicit DY year is retained even if it differs from the incident-derived TY; mismatches are counted here.

    Age is used as entered. Only if Age is blank is DOB read to calculate age on the incident date (or the upload date when undated). DOB is never displayed or saved. Years of service use PEBD/PEBP to Incident Date (or upload date when undated). Invalid and future dates are kept as unknown and counted. No SSN values are read.

    Rates: one selected TY, complete MSC/Battalion strength and no Duty Status filter are required. Per 1,000 = incidents ÷ assigned strength × 1,000. An MSC total overrides its battalion sum. A missing MSC total uses its battalions only if all have strength. Multi-year totals and duty-filtered records show counts only. Current TY is to-date, not annualized. Missing group strength shows “add strength”. Demographic cells show incident shares, never fabricated population rates.

    Peer comparison is a full-calendar-year Iowa administrative benchmark, not age-standardization, a causal inference, or a statistical test. Age/sex-specific rates and a full unit age roster were not available. No incident-only age mix is substituted for unit population weights.

    HOR populations are real 2020 Census counts for 942 incorporated Iowa places. Missing State means Iowa only if the town matches the Iowa lookup; otherwise Unmatched. Explicit non-IA state is always out of state, even if a same-named Iowa town exists. County shading is based on HOR town-center county—not incident location.
